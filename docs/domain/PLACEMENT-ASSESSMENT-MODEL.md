## 1. Recommended approach and boundaries

Use **fixed, stratified blueprints with constrained randomized forms**, objective marking and rubric-based human assessment in one owned placement domain. Prefer this over unrestricted random draws (unbalanced tests), one repeated fixed paper (exposure), or adaptive/IRT testing before sufficient calibration data exists. No adaptive engine, external testing platform, AI grading or new dependency is selected.

Every complete placement covers **vocabulary/language knowledge, grammar, reading, listening, speaking and writing**. Vocabulary and grammar may share a language-knowledge section, but retain separately attributable evidence and reporting. Tasks may carry secondary skill tags; allocate scored criteria explicitly so one mark is not counted twice. A large bank is maintained over time, not filled with unreviewed items to meet a numerical target.

**Prospect/Applicant → entrance assessment → reviewed current English level → justified course/level recommendation → separate admission → enrollment.** Program unknown: use a verified native Lead reference; use native Applicant when a real Program/year is meaningful. Returning learners reuse verified Student identity. Never create a fake Student/Enrollment to take a test. No placement action creates enrollment, native Academic Assessment Results, payroll or receivables.

Output is a **TOEFL House Internal Placement Result**, not official or mock TOEFL performance, CEFR certification or external certification. Academic course assessment remains native and separate. No default TOEFL sections, 0–30/0–120 scale or CEFR mapping. Recommended native Program/Course and internal level are references, not automatic admission or enrollment.

Prefer native identity, private-file, workflow and queue facilities where proven; keep domain-specific content/result authority owned. Preserve existing records by version/link, not destructive replacement.

## 3. Bank governance and scale

- Item/content lifecycle: **Draft → subject review → language/accessibility/key/rights review → approved/published → quarantined or retired**. Author cannot independently approve their own release. Keys, distractors, rubric anchors and media must be reviewed together. Published meaning is immutable; corrections create revisions.
- Controlled taxonomy: skill/subskill, internal target level, estimated difficulty, topic, format, stimulus/task family, language, mode, accessibility, expected effort, author/provenance and rights expiry. Primary category determines quota accounting; secondary tags cannot cause double-counting.
- Bulk imports enter staging with format/key validation, duplicate/near-duplicate review, media/rights checks and a rejection report. No import can publish automatically. Indexed metadata and eligible-pool queries support a large bank; do not load the entire bank into the browser or assume a bank-size/performance guarantee without load tests.
- Editorial difficulty is a hypothesis. Track item facility, discrimination where meaningful, distractor behavior, timing, mode, accommodation and cohort/blueprint versions. Small or biased samples must not automatically change difficulty or penalize learners. Academic review governs recalibration and retirement.
- A compromised/wrong item can be quarantined for future allocation immediately. Review affected attempts and releases explicitly; re-marking creates a new score/decision revision with reasons, never silently rewrites historical results. Historical evidence is access-controlled and retained under policy even after retirement, subject to lawful retention/holds.

## 4. Blueprint and question-selection model

1. **Publish a feasible blueprint.** Specify counts and point/time budgets per primary category: skill × internal level/difficulty band × allowed format, with topic coverage and stimulus dependencies. Validate that every supported mode has enough approved eligible items after exclusion/exposure rules. Reject contradictory quotas or incompatible accommodations; no runtime guesswork.
2. **Build an eligible pool server-side.** Exclude drafts, expired rights, quarantined items, inappropriate modes, duplicate/near-duplicate families, enemy items that reveal each other's answers, recent candidate exposures and exhausted item/stimulus exposure allowances. Identity uncertainty must not erase prior exposure history.
3. **Select under constraints, not independently per tag.** Stratified sampling without replacement within the attempt; balance difficulty profile, marks, task load and expected time. Keep stimulus/task groups together and avoid reusing their underlying passage/audio family on a retake merely by changing question IDs. Randomize allowed topic variants within quotas.
4. **Control exposure across candidates and retakes.** Use configurable per-window item/family/stimulus caps, candidate look-back exclusions and form-overlap limits. Prefer eligible lower-exposure items while preserving quotas. Reserve capacity atomically; prevent concurrent assignments overspending exposure limits. Count printed packets and delivered prompts as exposed even if the attempt is abandoned. Reclaim only demonstrably undelivered reservations under an audited policy.
5. **Freeze the manifest before delivery.** Use server-controlled unpredictable randomness; retain restricted selection provenance/seed and algorithm version plus the actual manifest for reproducibility. Do not disclose seeds, keys or future questions. Shuffle options only where meaning is preserved; remap answers by stable option identities. Do not shuffle an ordered sentence or dependent reading task blindly.
6. **Fail closed on shortage.** If exact constraints cannot be met, do not silently relax skill/difficulty/exposure limits. Defer allocation or use an already approved equivalent blueprint revision; log the shortage. Academic owners replenish/review the pool. Never invent placeholder questions.

This gives **controlled content comparability, not proven psychometric equivalence**. Equal category counts or raw percentages do not establish equal ability scales across forms/modes. Pilot/review difficulty and reliability before claiming interchangeable outcomes; use reviewed parallel forms and targeted reassessment when evidence is uncertain. Keep aggregate form statistics separate from a learner's permanent result. Exposure limits reduce repeatability; a finite bank cannot promise zero overlap forever.

## 5. Delivery, timing and evidence

| Mode | Objective components | Speaking | Writing | Evidence/control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Supervised digital** — recommended initial delivery setting | Candidate answers allocated items, including listening media, in the supported workflow | Recorded response **or** live assessor interaction through a qualified digital workflow; human rubric rating | Candidate types the task response | Scoped attempt access; response revisions, server deadlines and sealed evidence. Live unrecorded speaking uses assessor observations, not fictional audio evidence. |
| **Physical / staff-assisted** | Controlled printed packet or supervised media delivery; keyed responses may be entered for objective marking | In-person interview; assessor records criterion scores and observations | Paper script manually rated | Unique packet/manifest, script/page references, invigilator times, assessor identity and custody/return register; verify transcription before marking/release. Optional scans only under privacy policy. |
| **Hybrid** | Mode selected per section/component | Either supported speaking mode | Either supported writing mode | One attempt and policy, with mode/evidence explicitly attached to each component. Changes after start require an incident and approved continuation/replacement decision. |

**Digital identity boundary:** prefer a verified native User linked to the actual prospect/applicant and restricted to that candidate’s active placement. No Student profile is required and no parallel authentication provider is selected. Staff-assisted verification may initiate a sitting, but must never hand the candidate a staff session or staff credentials. Account claim/provisioning, recovery and learners without a usable unique email require a qualified identity path under M02; do not invent email addresses or assume native Student/Guardian guards cover prospect access.

All requested modes are part of the design, not separate scoring systems. Unsupervised remote delivery is not assumed equivalent to supervised testing; live digital interaction/media capture requires feasibility/security qualification before it is offered. No conferencing vendor, public portal framework or offline synchronization platform is selected.

**Timing:** blueprint defines section limits and an overall candidate-work budget, plus the allowed completion window for separate sessions. Configure these coherently; enforce the earliest applicable section/overall deadline. Server time governs digital starts, autosave acceptance, seal and timeout; a client timer is only a display. Assessor marking time is not candidate test time. Configure back-navigation and listening replay rules before publication. Replay/pauses must not give an unrecorded timing advantage.

Approved accommodations may change duration, breaks, input mode or task variant before allocation, with limited disclosure to relevant staff. They must preserve the assessed construct: a transcript is not automatically equivalent listening evidence, nor typed text equivalent speaking. Use an approved alternative assessment or qualified recommendation if equivalence cannot be justified. No silent score penalty for accommodation or physical mode.

Physical timing is invigilator-attested, not enforceable by a browser. Record interruptions, pauses and resumed deadlines consistently; later score entry does not create extra writing/interview time. Digital outages do not automatically pause clocks: preserve acknowledged responses and record the incident; authorized continuation or replacement follows policy. Do not backdate late submissions as timely.

## 6. Scoring, moderation and recommendations

| Response format | Recommended marking approach |
|---|---|
| Single-choice / true-false | Deterministic versioned answer key. |
| Multiple-select / matching / ordering | Explicit exact-match or bounded partial-credit rule; handle wrong selections and repeated matches consistently. Recommend no negative marking initially. |
| Fill-in-the-blank / bounded sentence-language task | Approved answer variants and narrowly defined case/whitespace/punctuation rules. Grammar/spelling may be the construct: do not apply broad fuzzy matching or autocorrect. Ambiguous legitimate answers go to review. |
| Open sentence, translation, explanation or other free response | Automatic marking only when correctness is unambiguously defined; otherwise human rubric. New formats require validated delivery and scoring contracts, not arbitrary executable question code. |
| Speaking / writing | Human analytic rubric, criterion evidence and rationale in all modes. No automatic AI/speech-transcription grade or inferred fluency score. |

Server computes objective scores from frozen keys, validated responses and scorer version. Responses and scores are separate authorities; clients cannot submit final scores. Define bounded units, precision, rounding, partial credit and missingness explicitly. A deliberate blank on a valid delivered item may receive the published zero rule; missing/corrupt/undelivered evidence is **not** silently zero or silently dropped from the denominator.

Report six skill profiles and completeness first. Normalize only compatible approved units; an optional overall internal composite requires explicit weights and a valid formula. Do not average descriptors or incomparable mode/form scores. Minimum-skill and uncertainty rules prevent strength in one skill from concealing inadequate speaking/writing. A composite, if approved, is not the sole course-placement criterion.

Recommended moderation: calibrated assessors and anchor examples; independent second review for borderline placements, disagreements, appeals, conflicts and integrity flags, plus a policy-defined sample of other human ratings. Blind second ratings to the first where feasible. A qualified reviewer adjudicates disagreements with reasons; never silently average incompatible ratings. Preserve required independent release approval; if no eligible independent reviewer is available, hold release.

Release requires verified subject, complete required evidence, valid timing/mode, completed marking/moderation and resolved material incidents. Output includes skill profile, internal level, evidence/policy/form versions, assessor/reviewer, rationale, validity and appropriate native course/level recommendation. Borderline/uneven or insufficient evidence produces **review required / targeted reassessment**, not a manufactured confident level. No automatic certification, admission or enrollment. Publish outcome feedback, not reusable answer keys or unrestricted recording/script access.

## 7. Lifecycle, retakes and integrity

- **Blueprint/policy:** Draft → reviewed/approved → published → retired. Publication freezes semantics; changes are new revisions.
- **Attempt:** Registered → allocated → identity/mode verified → in progress → submitted/timed out → marking → moderation → finalized. Interrupted/review-required is explicit; no-show, voided and invalid are separate outcomes, not low scores. A finalized attempt is not itself a released decision.
- **Incident:** preserve accepted responses, form exposure, timestamps, cause and actor. Authorized decisions are resume the same form within revised approved timing, invalidate/replace affected components with an equivalent form, or void and schedule a new attempt. Replacements are linked revisions/attempts, never overwritten evidence. Combining sessions/partial retakes requires an explicit freshness/comparability rule.
- **Decision:** Draft → reviewed → approved → released; corrections, supersession and revocation retain links and reasons. Latest reviewed, valid, released decision remains the effective recommendation. A newer incomplete/invalid attempt does not automatically replace it; invalidate an earlier decision only through its own reviewed action or policy expiry.
- **Retake:** new attempt after approved eligibility/wait; retain original outcomes and prevent repeated item/stimulus-family exposure within policy. Full reassessment is the default recommendation; targeted reassessment is permitted only where policy establishes that retained skill evidence is still valid. No automatic best-section/best-score combination.

Suspected cheating is an incident for human review, not proof from a single browser event. Randomization is not identity verification or proctoring. Protect keys/media/paper packets; supervise where appropriate; separate authors, publishers, assessors and release authority; log privileged access. Digital clients are untrusted: reauthorize subject/assignment/state on every read/write and queued operation, validate server timing and immutable allocation, reject stale/duplicate or cross-attempt writes, and reconcile idempotent retries. Media needs access-controlled upload/playback, size/type/content checks, completion/integrity verification and private storage; no public permanent recording URL. Capture/copying cannot be made impossible after authorized display or printing.

Frappe roles alone or a UI wrapper cannot contain native alternate writers. Existing Student/Guardian/File guards do not establish protection for these new parent types, digital candidate sessions, staff assignments or media. Document and qualify native CRUD/RPC/import/job/report/export/print/file/realtime paths, revocation, concurrency, offline/timeout incidents and recovery before acceptance. Keep all amendments auditable; hashes aid integrity checks but do not defeat privileged database operators or replace custody controls.
